If you are choosing between Meta Ads and Google Ads, you are really choosing between two different ways of finding customers. One shows your offer to people who were not looking for you. The other shows it to people who are searching for exactly what you sell.

Neither platform is better in every situation. This guide explains how each one works, what they cost to run, which suits which kind of business and how to test both without wasting budget. It is written for Indian business owners who want a clear answer, not a sales pitch.

## The short answer

Choose **Google Ads** when people already search for your product or service and each lead is worth a meaningful amount of money. Choose **Meta Ads** when you need to create demand, show something visual or reach a defined audience before they start searching.

Many businesses end up using both. Google captures people who are ready to act today. Meta keeps your brand in front of everyone else and brings back visitors who did not convert. The question is which one to start with, and the sections below help you decide.

## How each platform works

### Google Ads: you answer a question

Google Ads works mostly on **intent**. Someone types "interior designer in Powai" or "buy running shoes online", and your ad appears next to the results. The person is already looking, so the main job is to be relevant and to make it easy to enquire or buy.

Beyond Search, Google also offers YouTube video campaigns, Display banners and Performance Max, which mixes all of these using automation. For most small and mid-sized businesses, Search campaigns are the starting point because they are the easiest to measure and control.

### Meta Ads: you start the conversation

Meta Ads work mostly on **interest and behaviour**. They run across Facebook, Instagram, Messenger and WhatsApp, and they let you reach people by age, location, interests and past activity, or by lookalikes of your existing customers. Nobody searched for you, so the ad itself must catch attention.

That makes creative quality the biggest factor. A clear offer, a strong first few seconds of video and a simple call to action usually beat clever targeting. Our guide to [Meta Ads management](/services/meta-ads) covers the structure we use.

| Factor               | Google Ads                           | Meta Ads                              |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| Main mindset         | Searching for a solution             | Browsing and discovering              |
| Best for             | High-intent leads and sales          | Awareness, offers and visual products |
| Targeting            | Keywords, location, audience signals | Interests, behaviours, lookalikes     |
| Creative effort      | Text ads, simple assets              | Strong images and video are essential |
| Speed to first leads | Often very fast                      | Fast, but needs a learning period     |
| Cost per click       | Usually higher in competitive terms  | Usually lower per click               |
| Lead quality         | Often higher intent                  | Varies; needs good qualification      |
| Retargeting          | Yes                                  | Yes, and usually very effective       |

Treat this table as a general pattern, not a rule. A well-run Meta campaign can beat a badly run Google campaign, and the reverse is also true.

## When Meta Ads is the better choice

Meta Ads tend to work well in these situations:

- **Visual products and lifestyle brands.** Fashion, beauty, food, home decor and jewellery benefit from images and reels.
- **Local offers.** A salon, gym, cafe or coaching centre can promote a limited-time offer to people nearby.
- **Lead generation where people rarely search.** Courses, real estate launches and events often need to be introduced to the audience.
- **Low-priced, impulse-friendly products.** A lower cost per click suits products with small order values.
- **Retargeting.** Showing a reminder to people who visited your site or watched your video is one of the most efficient uses of budget.

Meta is also useful when your category has few searches. If nobody types your product name yet, a search ad has nothing to catch.

## When Google Ads is the better choice

Google Ads usually wins in these situations:

- **Clear, searchable demand.** Clinics, lawyers, CAs, repair services, movers and training institutes are searched for by name and need.
- **High-value leads.** When one customer is worth a lot, paying a higher cost per click is easy to justify.
- **Urgent needs.** People searching "AC repair near me" want help today.
- **B2B solutions.** Decision-makers search for software, vendors and services when they have a budget.
- **Ecommerce with strong product data.** Google Shopping and Performance Max can sell directly from your product feed.

## What each platform costs in India

Exact prices change by industry, season, city and competition, so be wary of anyone who quotes a single number for "Google Ads cost" or "Meta Ads cost". Here is what really drives cost.

**On Google Ads**, you pay when someone clicks. The price of a click is set by an auction. Competitive keywords, such as those in insurance, finance or real estate, cost more. Local, specific terms usually cost less. Your ad quality and landing page experience can also lower what you pay.

**On Meta Ads**, you usually pay for impressions or for results such as leads, depending on the objective. Cost depends on how large and competitive your audience is, how engaging your creative is and the time of year. Festive seasons and sale periods increase competition.

In both cases, the number that matters is not the cost per click. It is the **cost per lead** or **cost per sale**, and whether that is lower than what a customer is worth to you. ### A simple way to estimate your budget

Here is an illustrative example. The numbers are round, chosen only to show the method.

Imagine you sell a service for ₹20,000 and make a profit of ₹8,000 on each sale. You close one in four leads. That means you can afford to pay up to ₹2,000 for a lead, because four leads cost ₹8,000, which is your profit on a sale. If your clicks cost ₹40 and 5 in every 100 visitors become a lead, a lead costs ₹800 and the maths works. If clicks cost ₹150, a lead costs ₹3,000, and the same campaign loses money.

The lesson is to start from what a customer is worth, then work backwards. Our [digital marketing cost guide for India](/blog/digital-marketing-cost-india) explains this in more depth.

## Lead quality and the sales cycle

Google searchers often convert at a higher rate because they raised their hand. Meta leads can be cheaper but need stronger qualification: a form with one or two extra questions, a quick WhatsApp message or a phone call within minutes.

Speed matters on both platforms. A lead who is called within minutes is far more likely to talk than one who waits a day. If you generate leads, connect them to a system that replies instantly. Our [CRM setup and automation service](/services/crm-automation) is built for this.

## Creative and landing page needs

On Meta, the ad is the product. Plan for several creative angles, such as a problem-led video, a customer story and a direct offer, and refresh them as people tire of seeing them. Production matters, but authenticity often matters more than polish.

On Google, the ad text is short, so the landing page does much of the persuading. A page that matches the search, loads quickly on mobile and makes the next step obvious will lower your cost per lead. If your page is the weak link, a dedicated [landing page](/services/landing-pages-funnels) is often a better investment than more ad budget.

## Tracking: the part both platforms depend on

Both platforms learn from the conversions you report. If tracking is wrong, they optimise toward the wrong people. Before you spend on either platform:

1. Install and test the Meta Pixel and the Conversions API.
2. Set up Google Analytics 4 and Google Ads conversion tracking.
3. Track calls, form submissions and WhatsApp clicks, not just page views.
4. Agree how leads that become customers will be recorded.

## A simple 60-day test plan

If you are unsure where to start, run a controlled test.

**Weeks 1 and 2: set up.** Fix tracking, build one landing page and write several ad variations. Set a target cost per lead.

**Weeks 3 to 6: launch both, small.** Run one focused Google Search campaign and one Meta campaign, each with a modest daily budget. Keep structures simple and avoid changing too much at once.

**Weeks 7 and 8: compare and decide.** Look at cost per lead, lead quality and, most importantly, how many leads became sales. Move more budget to the platform that produces customers at an acceptable cost, and keep a smaller test running on the other.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Judging by clicks, not customers.** Cheap clicks that never convert are expensive.
- **Boosting posts instead of building campaigns.** Boosting offers limited goals and control.
- **Sending all traffic to the homepage.** A focused page usually converts better.
- **Changing everything every day.** Both platforms need time and data to learn.
- **Spreading a small budget across many campaigns.** Concentrate on your best offer first.
- **Ignoring follow-up.** The best ads cannot rescue slow replies.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Google Ads or Meta Ads cheaper?

Meta usually costs less per click, and Google usually brings higher-intent leads. The cheaper platform is the one with the lower cost per sale for your business, not the lower cost per click.

### Can I run both at the same time?

Yes. Many businesses use Google to capture active demand and Meta for awareness and retargeting. Start with one or two simple campaigns on each so you can learn what works.

### How long should I run ads before judging them?

Give each platform at least two to four weeks and enough budget to gather meaningful data. Judge on cost per qualified lead and sales, not on the first few days.

### Do I need an agency to run ads?

Not always, but ad accounts are easy to waste money in. An experienced team helps with structure, tracking and creative testing, and you should always own the ad account.
