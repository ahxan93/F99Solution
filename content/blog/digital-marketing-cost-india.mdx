"How much should I spend on digital marketing?" is the question every business owner asks first, and the honest answer is that it depends. Anyone who gives you one fixed number without knowing your goals is guessing.

What you can do is understand how pricing works, what drives it up or down and how to set a budget that makes sense for your business. This guide explains all three in plain language, with the costs expressed in rupees and the logic behind them, so you can judge any quote with confidence.

## The short answer

Digital marketing cost has two parts, and mixing them up is the most common budgeting mistake:

1. **Agency or specialist fees.** What you pay people to plan, create, manage and report.
2. **Media and tool costs.** What you pay platforms and software, such as ad spend on Meta and Google, email tools or a CRM.

Your fee for managing ads is separate from the ad budget itself. If an agency charges a monthly fee and you also spend a monthly amount on ads, your total marketing cost is both combined, plus GST.

A sensible budget starts from what a customer is worth to you, not from what other businesses spend. The sections below show how.

## What drives digital marketing cost

Five factors explain most of the difference between quotes.

**Scope.** Managing one social account costs less than running SEO, ads, email and a website together. The more channels and deliverables, the higher the cost.

**Competition.** Some markets are crowded. Ranking or advertising for terms in finance, real estate or healthcare takes more work and money than for a niche local service.

**Quality and experience.** Experienced specialists charge more per hour but often spend less of your budget on mistakes. Cheap work can be costly when it needs redoing.

**Starting point.** A site with technical problems, a broken ad account or no tracking needs fixing before growth starts.

**Speed and ambition.** Wanting results quickly, or in many cities at once, requires a larger investment.

## Common pricing models

You will meet four main models.

### Monthly retainer

You pay a fixed monthly fee for an agreed scope. This is the most common model for SEO, social media, content and ad management. It suits ongoing work and makes costs predictable.

### Percentage of ad spend

Some agencies charge a share of your advertising budget. It scales with spend, but it can reward higher spending instead of better results. If you choose this model, set a minimum and a cap, and judge results by cost per lead or sale.

### Fixed-price project

You pay a set amount for a defined outcome, such as a website, a logo or a CRM setup. It suits work with a clear start and end. Make sure the scope, number of revisions and timeline are written down.

### Performance-based

Fees depend on results, such as leads or sales. It sounds attractive, but results are affected by many things outside an agency's control, such as your offer, pricing and sales team. If you are offered this, ask exactly how a "result" is defined and who owns the data.

## Ad spend versus agency fee

Ad spend is paid directly to the platform from your own account, and it is not part of an agency's fee. This is how it should be: you keep control, visibility and ownership of the account.

Be careful with packages that bundle ad spend into one price. They make it hard to see how much goes to the platform and how much to the agency, and you may not own the account if you leave. At F99 Solution, ad accounts stay in your name and the ad budget is paid directly to Meta or Google.

## GST and other costs to remember

Agency services in India usually attract GST, currently 18% on most digital marketing services. Ask whether a quote includes GST, and confirm the rate with your accountant. Also remember the smaller costs: domain and hosting, email platforms, stock images, tools and licences.

## How to set a digital marketing budget

There are three practical ways to arrive at a number.

### Method 1: work backwards from customer value

This is the most reliable method for lead generation. Here is an illustrative example. The figures are round and only show the method.

Suppose you want 10 new customers a month. You close about one in five enquiries, so you need around 50 enquiries. If you can afford to pay ₹1,000 per enquiry, your monthly ad budget is ₹50,000. Add the management fee and any tools to see your full monthly cost. If one customer brings ₹30,000 in revenue and ₹10,000 in profit, then 10 customers bring ₹1,00,000 in profit, and the budget has a clear logic.

You can test your own numbers with our free [ad budget and ROI calculator](/tools/roi-calculator).

### Method 2: percentage of revenue

A common rule of thumb is to set aside a share of revenue for marketing, with the right figure varying by industry and growth stage. Use it as a starting guide, not a rule, and adjust it based on results.

### Method 3: the minimum effective budget

Ads need enough budget to gather data. If your budget buys only a handful of clicks a day, the platform cannot learn. In that case, narrow your focus to one offer and one location, and increase spend as results prove the model.

## What the first 90 days usually look like

Costs and results follow a pattern worth knowing:

- **Month 1:** setup and fixes. You pay for strategy, tracking, accounts, creative and technical work, and results are early.
- **Month 2:** testing. Ads show which offers and audiences work, while SEO and content groundwork continues.
- **Month 3:** optimisation. Budgets shift to what is working, and reporting starts to show trends.

Be wary of promises of instant, guaranteed results. Our [guide comparing Meta Ads and Google Ads](/blog/meta-ads-vs-google-ads-india) explains what to expect from paid channels.

## Red flags when comparing quotes

- **Guaranteed rankings or leads.** No one controls Google or Meta.
- **No written scope.** You should know exactly what you receive each month.
- **Vague reporting.** Insist on regular, plain-language reports with your own data.
- **The agency owns your accounts.** You should have admin access to ads, analytics and your domain.
- **Very low prices with large promises.** Quality work takes time.
- **Long lock-in contracts.** Look for reasonable notice periods, not multi-year commitments.

## Questions to ask before you hire

1. What exactly is included each month, and what is not?
2. How will you report results, and what will you measure?
3. Who owns the ad accounts, analytics and website?
4. Who will work on my account, and how often will we speak?
5. What does the first 90 days look like?
6. How do you handle changes in scope or budget?
7. What are the notice terms if we part ways?

## Frequently asked questions

### Is digital marketing worth it for a small business?

Yes, when it is aimed at a clear goal and measured against it. Start small with one channel and one offer, and expand as you see results.

### Can I start with a small budget?

Yes. Begin with the channel closest to a sale, often local search or a focused ad campaign, and use the data to decide where to invest next.

### How long before I see results?

Paid ads can bring leads within weeks. SEO and content take months to build, but their results continue to grow.

### Should I choose the cheapest quote?

Choose the best value, not the lowest price. Compare scope, experience, reporting and account ownership, and confirm what the price includes.
